# Shagun — voice scripts (ASSETS.md §3)

Every line the chapter speaks, split by actor, ready to hand to five friends.
Each actor needs only their own section, plus "How to record". The lines are
the chapter's own (content/shagun); don't change the words. Pauses, breaths
and stumbles are welcome.

**Spoilers:** Sameer's actor reads the whole twist. Cast someone who won't be
playing it fresh.

## How to record

- **On a phone, as a WhatsApp voice note** or in the phone's voice recorder.
  Not a studio mic: it has to sound like a phone because it is one. Hold it
  where you'd hold it for a voice note, and don't cup it.
- **A room with a little life in it.** A fan or distant traffic is good. Music,
  TV and other voices aren't. Raju records outdoors, near a road.
- **One file per recording, named as below.** Three takes of each, with a clap
  or silence between takes, is ideal. We'll pick.
- **Aim for the length given.** It's what the phone shows on screen. A second
  either side is fine.
- **Send the originals** (m4a, opus or wav) to Siddhant. Don't convert or
  clean them up.
- Nobody says their own real name, and nothing real gets said about anyone.

Once the files arrive, they go in `public/found/shagun/voice/`. The player then
hears them where they read captions now. Voice notes, memos, calls and clips
need audio playback added; that's code, not a recording job.

this number 47 times. Every call is hope first.

**Call 1, Saturday 11:41 PM.** The player answers a stranger's phone.

## 3. Vinod Bhasin, 58: 1 call (5 recordings)

Retired police officer, now the Sehgal family's "security and liaison".
Everyone's *Bhasin uncle*. **Never raises his voice.** Polite (*aap*,
*Neelam ji*), unhurried, faintly amused. The menace comes from how
reasonable he sounds. Delhi Punjabi undertone.

**Sunday 2:05 AM**, to whoever has the phone.

| File | Line | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `bhasin-open` | *Hello.* … *Uske phone ki location uski maa ke phone pe dikhti hai. Mujhe bhi.* … *Subah tak courier waale ko wapas de dena. Baaki hum dekh lenge.* (His phone's location shows on his mother's phone. And to me. Give it back to the courier by morning. We'll see to the rest.) | Like a man arranging a car. *Mujhe bhi* is the threat, so give it nothing extra. |
| `bhasin-neelam` | *Aap Sameer nahi ho. Neelam ji ne bata diya tha.* (You're not Sameer. Neelam ji told me.) | Recorded separately: said only if the player told Sameer's mother they aren't Sameer. Warm, almost kind. |
| `bhasin-theek` | *Samajhdaar ho.* (Sensible.) | Approving, like a teacher. |
| `bhasin-police` | *Kaunsi police? Saket wali?* … *Theek hai. Location hamare paas hai.* (Which police? The Saket ones? Fine. We have the location.) | Genuinely amused by *police*, then patient. |
| `bhasin-kyun` | *Aapko nahi pata us raat kya tha.* … *Phone wapas kar do. Aapke liye achha hoga.* (You don't know what that night was. Give the phone back. It'll be better for you.) | The only line with any weight in it, and it's still quiet. |

## Sounds (not actors)

These can come from free, no-attribution libraries (Pixabay Sound Effects,
Freesound's CC0 section), or be recorded on a phone:

- dhol at a baraat, 10–15 s
- two celebratory shots in the air, **distant, not cinematic**, and one shot
  straight up on an open lawn
- a crowd cheering at night, and laughter
- footsteps on gravel, a plastic jerrycan set down
- fire crackle, wind across smoke
- road traffic and bike wind (under Raju's calls)
