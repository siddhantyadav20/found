Found · Raakh · design study

What Raakh’s case file should be

Fifteen detective and deduction games, plus Candy Crush and Duolingo, read for one question: what makes logging a case feel like detective work instead of filling in a questionnaire. Each finding is turned into a decision for Raakh.

12 Nov 2026 · Raakh as of commit 8f3364a · the game details are from play knowledge and the sources at the end

The verdict
  1. The player makes the deduction; the game only checks it. Every game that feels like detective work makes the player commit to something the game hasn’t already decided (Obra Dinn, Golden Idol, Ace Attorney, Orwell). Raakh’s rows fill themselves, and that is the main reason it reads as a form.
  2. The verb is “pin this clue to that gap”. It’s Ace Attorney’s present evidence, scaled down to a phone. The clue you pin earns its word in the sentence, as Golden Idol’s collected words do. It’s one tap more than today, and that tap is the deduction.
  3. Keep the choice small, and keep a wrong pin free. Show the last two or three clues you saw, not the whole evidence list. A wrong pin gets the pointed line already written in refuse and costs nothing. This is the lesson from Golden Idol’s “close” indicator and from our own playtests, where open sentence-building was a wall.
  4. Make Sameer’s account a real rival. Once the rows are in, the player calls it between his version and theirs. Choosing his tells you which row it breaks on (Orwell’s conflicting data, Golden Idol’s yellow).
  5. Help shows before it tells. Candy Crush wiggles a valid move when you idle. Raakh should glow the clue that fits, or the app it’s in, before any written hint, and wait longer for a player who has been quick (Duolingo’s Birdbrain aims for middle difficulty).
  6. A filed line changes the note. Pencil becomes print with a stamp (Obra Dinn). The line takes its place on a timeline of the night, and the next unknown hour is visible, like the next level on a saga map.
  7. Between sessions, the characters are the notification. One opt-in message in a character’s voice about a day after the last play, rotated so it never repeats, and it stops after two are ignored (Duolingo, Lifeline). No lives, energy or paid skips.
Today

Why the current case file reads like a form

I opened it mid–Episode 2 on Q5. Four things give it a questionnaire feel. None of them is the visual style.

  • The game deduces for you. Open the right message and the row writes its own conclusion (“At 1:14 Dilip was awake”). The player’s only act is pressing File on a sentence they didn’t form.
  • It counts like a form. “Episode 2 · Question 2 of 3” and “1 of 3 shown so far” measure completion, not understanding.
  • The question is compound. “Was Dilip alive, and what happened to the car that came for him?” is two questions with a comma, like an exam item.
  • Answers vanish into a list. “Answered” is a stack of finished items. It doesn’t build a picture of the night, so filing doesn’t feel like it changed anything.

The data under it is already right. Each row is a fact with routes in by. Q5 already has two wrong answers with pointed replies (“At 2:41 he’d stopped speaking… Look earlier.”, “Who wrote ‘Dilip ko service gate pe le aao’?”). And say.learn already maps each word to the clue that earns it. The rebuild is mostly what the player does with this data, not new data.

The games

Fifteen case files, and what each one teaches

I grouped them by what their case file is: a book you complete, a confrontation you win, or a body of data you sort. For each, the one thing worth carrying into Raakh.

Books and boards: the case file is a thing you complete

Return of the Obra Dinn

2018 · Lucas Pope

A logbook with 60 blanks: who each person is and how they died. Fates are confirmed only in sets of three, so you can’t guess one at a time. Confirmed entries turn from handwriting into print.

TakeConfirmation that changes the artifact. Raakh’s filed lines should go from Sameer’s pencil to print, and stay there.

The Case of the Golden Idol

2022 · Color Gray Games

You collect words by clicking things in a frozen scene, then drag them into the blanks of an account. When a section is one or two words off, it turns yellow instead of red.

TakeWords are earned from evidence, and being close is rewarded. Here the clue you pin earns the word. Calling Sameer’s version says which part fails. The developers say the yellow indicator made the game shorter and easier, so we use it once, at “Call it”, not on every row.

Heaven’s Vault

2019 · inkle

You translate an ancient script by guessing a word from a few options. A guess stays tentative until the word turns up in enough other inscriptions, then it’s confirmed.

TakeHunches that firm up later. Raakh already files some lines “as your hunch”. Show them in pencil, and ink them when a later clue backs them.

Sherlock Holmes: Crimes & Punishments

2014 · Frogwares

The Deduction Space links clues into nodes, and some nodes allow more than one conclusion. The game flags contradictions but doesn’t say whether you’re right until you commit. Then you choose to absolve or condemn.

TakeCompeting conclusions from the same clues, and a moral call at the end. That’s Raakh’s Call it, and its endings.

Shadows of Doubt

2023 · ColePowered

A free-form corkboard with pins and strings. String colour and width show how incriminating and reliable a link is. Cases are closed on a Resolve screen of objectives you pick answers for.

AvoidBoth halves. The board turns into a tangle even on a monitor, and Resolve is exactly the questionnaire we’re leaving.

Outer Wilds

2019 · Mobius Digital

No quest log. The ship log shows what you know as a map of entries, and marks the ones with more to find. Knowledge is the only progression.

TakeShow the gaps without the answers. A dashed “what I don’t know” row works like Outer Wilds’ “more to explore” marker.

Confrontations: the case file is ammunition

Ace Attorney

2001– · Capcom

The Court Record holds evidence. In cross-examination you press a witness, then present the one item that contradicts a statement. A wrong present costs credibility.

TakePresent evidence against a specific claim, which is the verb we said we wanted. Drop the penalty: Raakh is about understanding, not a health bar.

Danganronpa

2010– · Spike Chunsoft

In Nonstop Debate, statements scroll past with weak points highlighted. You fire a Truth Bullet (a piece of evidence) at the one that’s false.

TakeSameer’s account as a statement with a weak point you hit with a clue. That’s a stretch goal for the final question, where his whole version is on trial.

L.A. Noire

2011 · Team Bondi

Interrogation options were Truth, Doubt and Lie, and Lie needs evidence. Players misread “Doubt” as an aggressive accusation, and the 2017 release relabelled them Good Cop, Bad Cop and Accuse.

TakeMake the option label say what happens. In Call it, each choice is the full sentence you’d file, never an abstract label.

Paradise Killer

2020 · Kaizen Game Works

A free investigation with no quest log for much of development. You start the trial when you choose, accuse whoever you can make a case against, and the verdict is whatever you can prove.

TakeThe ending is the player’s case. Raakh’s final question should read as Sameer arguing his own trial, not a last quiz item.

Data and phones: the case file is what you make of someone’s data

Orwell

2016 · Osmotic Studios

You drag “datachunks” from chats and web pages onto a suspect’s profile. Some chunks conflict, and you must choose which goes to the government. There’s no game over for choosing wrong, only consequences. Early tests showed that without drawbacks, players just dragged everything that was highlighted.

TakeConflicting versions are where the decisions are, and filing has to cost something to mean anything. In Raakh the cost is the record: what you file is what Sameer has said.

Her Story

2015 · Sam Barlow

You search a police video database, and each search shows only the first five clips. Your own notebook is the only case file. You decide when you understand enough.

TakeSlow discovery on purpose, and let the player say “I know enough”. Raakh’s search and “Leave the case” already do this. The note shouldn’t list evidence the player hasn’t found.

Simulacra

2017 · Kaigan Games

The whole game is a missing woman’s phone: her apps, chats and photos. There’s no game interface outside the phone. Replica and Sara Is Missing use the same frame.

TakeStay inside the fiction. Raakh’s case file is Sameer’s own Notes note, and the twist depends on it. The redesign must still look like a note on his phone, not a game screen.

Lifeline

2015 · 3 Minute Games

A stranded astronaut texts you in real time. When he’s busy, he goes quiet for hours, and a push notification brings you back when he writes again. Duskwood later took the messenger frame into an episodic murder mystery.

TakeCharacters are the notification. If Raakh ever reaches out between sessions, it should be Nitin or Mummy writing, not “Found misses you”.

Disco Elysium

2019 · ZA/UM

The Thought Cabinet holds ideas you’re “internalising”, which take time and change you. Your skills talk to you as inner voices, commenting on what you see.

TakeThe case file has a voice. Sameer’s note can talk back to the player’s pins in short lines, which is what the refuse replies already are.

The pattern

What the good ones share

Across all fifteen, deduction feels like deduction when four things are true at once:

  • The player commits to a claim the game hasn’t made for them: a name, a fate, a word in a blank, a piece of evidence.
  • It’s backed by something they chose: the scene they clicked, the item they presented, the chunk they dragged.
  • There is a tempting alternative: a conflicting chunk, a near-miss word, a witness’s version.
  • The world answers back: the witness breaks, the entry turns to print, the government acts on it.

And the failures share three things: an open board that gets messy (Shadows of Doubt), a choice set so big you brute-force it (Golden Idol’s late scrolls, as reviewers note), and silence about whether you’re close. Raakh’s current file has the opposite problem. It is so guided that there’s nothing to commit to.

Candy Crush and Duolingo

The logic under the habit

Both games are built so the next move is always obvious and close, and so coming back takes almost no effort. Here is what each does, and what Raakh should do with it. Take means use as is, adapt means change it to fit a story, and avoid means it works against a one-time narrative game.

1

A lead, not “Question 2 of 3”. The heading is the time window and place the question is about, and the question is one short sentence in Sameer’s voice. The compound question splits into its rows.

Outer Wilds, Her Story

2

Gaps are the rows. Each fact shows as a dashed, pencilled gap using its what (“who sent the car”). Known rows are in ink, with the clue pinned beside them: its source and time in mono.

Obra Dinn, Outer Wilds

3

Pin a clue to a gap. What you’ve seen on the phone lands in “Just seen”: the two or three most recent clues that bear on this lead, plus at most one that doesn’t. Tap a clue, then a gap. If it’s on one of that fact’s routes, the gap inks itself. If not, the note says why in one line (the refuse text, such as “At 2:41 he’d stopped speaking. That isn’t the same thing. Look earlier.”) and the clue goes back. No penalty.

Ace Attorney, Golden Idol, Orwell

4

Call it. When every gap is filled, the note offers the sentences that fit: the line your pins make, and, where it’s available, Sameer’s own account, stamped “His account”. Calling his says which row it breaks on. Filing turns pencil to print with a stamp.

Orwell, Sherlock C&P, Golden Idol, Obra Dinn

5

The night so far. Filed lines sit at their hour. An account later disproved is struck in red. The next unknown hour shows as a “?”. That is your progress, in the story’s own units.

Candy Crush saga map, Sherlock C&P

6

Help shows first. On a pause, the fitting clue wiggles (as above). If nothing in the tray fits, the app it’s in pulses in the dock. Then come the written hints we already derive from the script. The pause scales with the player’s own pace.

7

Risk

The one thing that could go wrong

Pinning puts back an act that playtesters stalled on before: the old sentence-building flow, and “tabling proof”. This design differs in three ways. The choice is two or three recent clues instead of every word in a sentence. A wrong pin costs nothing and says why. And the glow arrives before the player is stuck long enough to quit.

It still needs measuring. I’d log every pin (right or wrong, which row, seconds since the last one) to /insights. The target is a median of about one wrong pin per question. Near zero means it’s too easy and we’re back to a form. Above two or three, the tray is too big or the clues are unclear.

Coming back

Between sessions

Our 20-player run had people stop mid–Episode 2. Bringing them back is a separate problem from the case file, and it comes second.

  • End each episode on a message arriving. Reopening the game should land on the lock screen with that message waiting, so the first thing on return is the story, not a menu.
  • Opt-in return messages. At the end of an episode, offer “Let Nitin text you when he writes back”. Send one message about a day later at the time they last played, in a character’s voice. Rotate four or five so nobody gets the same one twice, and stop after two are ignored.
  • The technical catch. Found has no web app manifest or service worker today, so web push needs both. On iPhone, web push only works after the player adds the site to their home screen (iOS 16.4 and later). Email is the fallback for everyone else. I’d build this only after the case file is tested.
Build order

What I’d build, in order

  • 1. The note. A rebuild of the lines case file in components/stage/CaseFile.tsx: lead heading, gaps, the clue tray, pin, Call it, stamps, and the night timeline. In the engine, a row fills when a clue on one of its routes is pinned to it, saved as a flag. Filing still goes through answer(), so endings, pays and struck versions keep working. Story mode keeps today’s auto-fill.
  • 2. Help that shows. The glow on the fitting clue or app, timed from the player’s own pace, then the derived text hints.
  • 3. The checks. The script checker learns the new rules (every row has a route that isn’t a decoy, and every version claim names the row it breaks on). The follower bot learns to pin, so it still proves every ending is reachable by following the help.
  • 4. Measure, then people. Pin events on /insights, then three to five people play by touch on a phone. Their results decide the tray size and the glow timing.
  • 5. Return messages. Only after the note is right.
