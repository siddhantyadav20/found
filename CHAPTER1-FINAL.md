# FOUND — Chapter One: "Low Battery" · Mumbai
## Locked build document (v3, final)

FOUND is an anthology. Each chapter is one city, one object, one story, three
episodes, finished. Nothing carries over but the shape of the thing, and the
shape is the product: **you are handed a stranger's device, and everything you
open to help them becomes evidence.**

Chapter One is Mumbai, and the object is a phone.

- **Episode 1 — "Don't Unlock It."** ~30 min
- **Episode 2 — "Read Receipts"** ~30 min
- **Episode 3 — "Delivered"** ~35 min

# PART 2 — The truth

Shree Ram Mills, Lower Parel, shut since 2009, is held up by one heritage
listing on its 1923 engine house. A listed building can't be demolished; one
damaged beyond repair can be delisted and cleared. Anand Realty wants it gone,
and their fixer **Kiran Shetty** is paid to arrange an electrical fire for
Sunday night.

He hires **{name} Sethi, 19**, who photographs shut buildings at night, to shoot
the interior first — the client likes to know what they're buying. What the
client actually needs is where the engine sits and where the original wiring
runs, so the fire looks like it started by itself. **She isn't documenting a
crime. She's drawing the plan.** And a student's night photographs of that
interior, taken two days before a fire, are a scapegoat for ₹15,000.

**Friday.** She leaves Tara's party at 22:02, argues with Dev outside, searches
Maps for Gate 3 at 22:24, walks 4.1 km. No signal inside, so at 23:36 the phone
joins the crew's own router, SRM-GATE3-GUEST. At 23:38 she photographs a white
van that shouldn't be there. Then she switches to NightCam — cloud only, nothing
kept on the phone — and shoots **twelve frames between 23:39 and 23:50**.

What she doesn't understand she has photographed: petrol already stacked inside,
a sprinkler head taped over, the wiring above the engine bay — and at **23:47**,
at the edge of a torch beam, two men carrying something wrapped out of the
engine house.

**Ramesh Shinde, sixty-one, the night watchman.** Kiran had told her the guard
was off till midnight. He wasn't. He came back, and he found them.

23:52, a voice memo: footsteps, a train horn very close, a held breath —
*"someone's here."*

**00:05 Saturday.** Kiran takes the phone, makes her unlock it, finds the van
photo in Recents and deletes it at 00:07. There is nothing else on the phone to
find. He turns location sharing on to himself, switches it off, and locks her in
the pump room — because she is no longer a girl who photographed a van. She is a
witness to a body. She gets out through the drain culvert on Saturday night,
walks the tracks to Currey Road, and reaches Tara's.

**Sunday 23:48, the mill burns**, with Shinde inside it. The fire clears a
heritage listing *and* explains a death.

**Monday 07:40.** Kiran, driving out of the city, drops the phone at 4% into a
letterbox 14 km away — the first building past the toll — photographs the
letterboxes so he knows which flat, and texts it from a burner: *"Don't unlock
it."*

## Why a stranger got it

He can't reach the cloud, so he ruins the container. **Evidence an unidentified
person has had for three days is evidence no prosecutor will touch**, and Mum's
Guardian app — a mother's own account — produces the timestamped proof that a
stranger was inside it.

**Why *this* stranger: no reason.** You were the nearest letterbox to the road
out. The player can prove it — his route, the toll, the time, a row of
letterboxes photographed so he'd know which one. Being chosen would be
flattering. Being available is worse.

## Who is lying

**Tara** is hiding her and lies from Friday night on. **Mum** saw Friday's
Guardian entries on Saturday morning and told nobody, because she suspected her
own daughter. **Dev** won't say the argument was about K. **Kiran** believes he
is running this. **3107** is her, unconfirmed until Episode 3. And **the news
says *no casualties*** — the biggest lie in the chapter, and the player is the
one who breaks it.

# PART 5 — Episode 3: "Delivered" · ~35 min

## Act 1 — What she photographed (~15 min)

The eleven remaining frames download one at a time, paced by what the player
does, never by a real clock.

- **Frame 3** a sprinkler head, taped. **Frame 4** the wiring above the engine
  bay, lit and centred — *why photograph a ceiling?*
- **Frame 5** a clipboard: **ENGINE HOUSE · HERITAGE CONDITION · …1923…**
- **Frames 6–9** Gate 3, the van's plate, a man unloading, wet tracks following
  her in.
- In **Notes**, her unsent draft: *"ask K what clearance means"* / *"if this is
  just photos why does he care what survives."*
- In **Dabba**, a food order near Currey Road on Saturday night. She got out.
  Nobody says so.

## Act 2 — The guard (~8 min)

**Frame 11, 23:47.** A hand over the lens. Behind it: two men carrying something
wrapped out of the engine house. Nothing graphic — a shape at the edge of a
torch beam. **The only pinch-zoom in the game**, so it's the player's own hand
that finds him.

Then the plant, from Episode 1, pays: Kiran's line in the vault — *"The guard is
off till 12."* And in City Desk's local column on Saturday, four lines nobody
read: a watchman's family have reported him missing.

**Q6 — Who was carried out of the engine house on Friday night?**
Answer from the phone: **Ramesh Shinde**, sixty-one.

*Hints:* someone was supposed to be off duty that night → Kiran told her the
guard was off till midnight → the guard came back; find what the twelve saw at
23:47.

Everything re-reads. She wasn't locked in a pump room over a photograph of a
van. 3107 isn't hiding from an arson charge. And Sunday's fire, in a building
that had to be damaged beyond repair anyway, is also where a body could be
found without anyone asking when he died.

## Act 3 — Two clocks and a call (~10 min)

**He is coming.** Kiran turns **location sharing** back on — the switch the
player may have touched in Episode 1 — and the phone shows two dots on a map,
converging. *(If the player turned sharing off back then, he can't: instead he
sends a photograph of their stairwell. Same dread, earned differently.)* Then
the third and last message he ever sends, using their name: **"I'm outside.
Bring it down."**

**She is walking.** 3107: *"the police want me. i'm going to tell them what i
saw."* She is outside Byculla station. If she walks in now, the only proof she
has of anything is on a phone a stranger has been inside for three days.

**Then the phone rings.** The first and only call in the chapter. The player
cannot type. They can listen — room tone, a train going past behind her, a voice
that has been a whisper for two and a half episodes and is suddenly a person —
and say one of three things.

> **"I'm going in. Tell me not to."**

# PART 6 — The ending

**What you say is the ending.** Then you have sixty seconds to do it, while
someone knocks.

## "Go in. I'm sending them everything."

The upload bar is the last thing the player watches. Twelve frames, the router
they went through, the vault, and Mum's Guardian report attached as chain of
custody — which names the player, because that is the only thing that makes the
rest admissible.

Shinde's death is reopened as culpable homicide. Kiran and two others are
arrested. The delisting halts. **She is cleared.**

The player is a witness in a homicide case and every private thing they opened
is in the file. The last message is a journalist asking for their name.

## "Don't go in. Run."

You wipe the phone while she walks away from the station. No device, no custody,
no case — and the case *against her*, built almost entirely out of a stranger's
activity, collapses with it.

Nobody is charged. The fire stays an accident. She is free, and gone. **Anjali
Sethi keeps calling a number that doesn't ring**, and Shinde's family are told
he was sleeping somewhere he shouldn't have been.

## "Don't go in. I'll fix it."

The kind answer. You open the door and hand it over, because that is the only
way to make him leave.

He wipes it; the account dies with the device. She waits for a call that doesn't
come, and is charged over the mill fire — no photographs, only a story about a
night she was paid to be there. Shinde's family receive a payment from a
contractor's insurer.

**Final screen:** four months on, one news item — the engine house delisted, the
site cleared. Then the desk, and the envelope is gone from it too. **You were
never in danger. That was the arrangement.**

## The last twenty seconds (all three)

Tara, once: *"she said you'd know what to do."*

Then the desk. The lamp finds one of the dim shapes that has been sitting at the
edge of the light since the very first screen — and it isn't a phone.

> **Someone in Delhi is about to lose something.**
> **Next Friday.**

No cliffhanger. No withheld answer. No purchase prompt.

# PART 7 — The confusion fix (still the top priority)

The one piece of real player feedback we have is that people didn't know what to
do. The lock screen is fine — it teaches itself. The failure is the ten seconds
*after* the phone unlocks. Five mechanisms, all in fiction, no tutorial pop-ups:

1. **Badges on app icons.** A badge means *unseen evidence*, not unread
   messages, so Health and Settings carry them too. Ten identical icons become a
   lit path; no badges left means look at the case file.
2. **One open question at a time**, in the case file and on the home widget.
3. **"Where to look: Health · Settings · Photos"** under every question. Names
   apps, never answers, costs nothing.
4. **The Found counter** — *Added to case file · 7 found* — so opening things is
   visible progress.
5. **The idle nudge.** Forty-five seconds with nothing found and the phone
   buzzes with a hint from the case file, escalating one tier each time.

**First sixty seconds:** envelope → *"Don't unlock it."* → Medical ID → unlock →
one buzz → **Case file · You started a note** → Q1 with where-to-look → home
screen with Messages badged 3, Photos badged 2, everything else quiet.

**Documents:** Guardian's report · the custody log, generated from the player's

# PART 9 — Product, game, motion, sound

**Product.** Chapter One free, all three episodes. From Chapter Two, ₹49 a
chapter or ₹149 a month, with every chapter's Episode 1 free. Case numbers
restore saves on any device *(built)*. The desk shows your phone as you left it
*(built)*. A result card with your ending and one comparison line — *1 in 4
players said what you said*. Pass-it-on, spoiler-safe, WhatsApp first, and the
end card says what it is: **"Someone passed this to you. Pass it on."**

**Game.** State is flags, as built. One puzzle type per beat, never the same
twice running: cross-app deduction, temporal contradiction, visual forensic,
typed answer, social deduction, interpretation. Every question has two
independent routes and a three-tier hint ladder. Four irreversible acts — open
Mum's thread, turn receipts off, recover the deleted photo, threaten Kiran —
each changes later lines, headlines, how Kiran reaches you at the climax, and
who speaks for you at the end. No branching content; state changes what existing
content says.

**Motion.** Kept: the desk phone morphing into the envelope, apps zooming out of
their icons, flickable banners, the lock screen lifting. New: **the report
leaving** — Guardian's notification holds one beat too long, then the phone's
real power-off collapse; **the download** — thumbnail first, sharpening as it
lands; **frame 11's pinch-zoom**, the only one in the game; **the two dots**
converging on a map; **the call** — full-bleed, no apps, one name, ninety
seconds; **the desk return**, where the lamp finds the next object. All of it
respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Sound.** The buzz is the franchise. Silence is the instrument: the typing
indicator that stops, the sync that halts at 11 of 12, the held breath in the
memo. Ordinary sounds for terrible moments. Real voices for everyone except
{name}, who stays a whisper *until the call* — which is exactly why it lands.
Fully playable silent, with captions on every memo and on the call.

# PART 10 — The chapter template (for Delhi, and after)

What makes a FOUND chapter, so the next one can be built to the same shape:

1. **An object that keeps a record.** A phone, a dashcam, a camcorder, a fitness
   band. It must log time, place and use without being asked.
2. **A person who is missing, and a stranger holding their life.**
3. **A crime the object witnessed by accident**, which its owner didn't
   understand they had recorded.
4. **A third party who watches the player** — a parent's app, a fleet tracker,
   a school portal, an insurer's box. The thing that turns curiosity into
   evidence. This is the franchise, not the plot.
5. **Three episodes:** a person you come to like; a turn that makes the player
   complicit; a finale where a real voice asks the player for something.
6. **A local truth that only works in that city.** Mumbai: mill land and a
   heritage listing. Delhi: whatever is only true in Delhi.
7. **An ending that closes**, three ways, none clean, all costing something.
8. **The last twenty seconds return to the desk**, and light the next object.

# PART 12 — North star

**Feel:** that looking is never free.

**Tell a friend:** *"Don't Google anything. And plug your phone in before
Episode 2."*

**Fear:** that the record of what you did is more complete than your memory of
doing it.

**Realise:** a sixty-one-year-old man came back to work on Friday, and the only
proof of what happened to him is on a phone that was handed to a stranger so it
would stop counting.

**Come back because:** next Friday there is a different object in a different
city, and you know exactly how good this felt.

**Never lose:** the moment the player understands that the crime scene has a
record of *them*.
