# Why v5 fails, and what the Raakh trailer has to be

12 Nov 2026. Written after watching v5 frame by frame and reading its soundtrack's spectrogram, against v4 and against the research in `TRAILER-RESEARCH.md`.

## 1. The verdict on v5

v5 is a screen recording with a slow zoom. It is not a trailer.

| What the frames show | Seconds | Share |
|---|---|---|
| A phone's UI, read like a document (chats, poster, case file, lock screen) | 46 | 54% |
| Black, with a line of text | 19 | 22% |
| A vertical phone video pillarboxed inside a widescreen frame | 12 | 14% |
| Photographed imagery filling the frame (the montage) | 5 | 6% |
| Title and end card | 10 | 12% |

Three things are wrong, and none is a detail:

1. **There is no world.** No light that moves, no face, no room, no weather, no depth. For 85 seconds the camera never leaves a black desk. Cinema is photographed space; v5 has none. v4, for all its faults, had a venue from the air, sparklers, a dance floor, fog, a road, a pyre, a face lit by a screen. Every one of its seconds had light in it.
2. **The music is not there.** The spectrogram shows it: after the dhol stops at 19 s, the next 35 seconds carry almost nothing above 100 Hz except bursts of voice. The drone and the heartbeat are sub-bass, which a laptop or a phone cannot reproduce at all. A viewer on a MacBook hears the gunshot, then silence with occasional talking, then a noise sweep. There is no harmony, no melody, no mid-range, no pulse you can feel. A synthesised sketch replaced a score.
3. **The voices are synthetic and exposed.** Six text-to-speech lines carry the story. Inside the game, behind a tap, they pass. In a trailer, where a stranger decides in five seconds whether this is real, they read as cheap. The research said one voice should carry the premise. It has to be a real one.

The lesson from the research was misread. "The product's form is the opening" meant *The Social Network* spent sixty seconds on Facebook's interface, then cut to a film. It did not mean the film is the interface. The phone is the hook, not the picture.

## 2. What the trailer has to be

**A film about a phone, not a phone.** Real, photographed night: a Delhi farmhouse wedding, then a dark flat at 3 AM, a phone the only light in it. The game's screens appear as inserts, no more than ten seconds in total, each one motivated by a shot of a real hand, a real face, a real desk. Everything else is cinematography and sound.

**One rule per department:**

- **Camera.** Every shot has a light source inside it (fairy lights, a sparkler, a phone screen, a lamp, a fire, a street light) and a reason for the camera to be there. Slow push-ins, shallow focus, handheld only when someone in the shot is afraid. No stills with Ken Burns pans. Vertical material is never pillarboxed: it is cropped into the 2.39 frame, or it is shown on a phone that sits in a wider shot.
- **Music.** One real cue with harmonic content, chosen before the cut, and the picture cut to its hits. Dark strings and piano under a slow pulse, with Indian colour (a tanpura drone, a single sarangi or bansuri line) so it belongs to this story and not to every other trailer. The dhol is diegetic: it belongs to the wedding, it dies with the gunshot.
- **Sound.** Layered like a film: the room (a ceiling fan, a fridge, traffic far away, a dog), the object (cloth, the phone's buzz on wood, a thumb on glass), the moment (the shot, the ears ringing, breath). Silence is used three times and earned each time.
- **Voice.** One real voice reads the premise in Hinglish, close and quiet, as a voice note. Record it on a phone in a quiet room: a friend, or you. Three lines at most. No synthetic voices anywhere in the cut.
- **Text.** Five cards, not seventeen. They carry what a voice cannot: the dates and the clock.

## 4. The gameplay in it

The game appears three ways, in this order of importance:

1. **Filmed.** The real game on a real phone in a real hand, in the flat shots. This is the only way the UI looks like cinema: the glass reflects the room, the thumb is a real thumb, focus breathes. Six shots, one evening, an iPhone on a tripod and a lamp. The list is in section 6.
2. **Composited.** On a locked-off stock shot of a phone lying on a table, the game's actual screen can be placed on the phone with a perspective warp. Two shots at most.
3. **Inserted.** Full-frame punch-ins of the real screen, one to two seconds each, always cut from a hand or a face. The v5 compositor can render these crisply at any size; that is the one part of v5 worth keeping.

Total UI on screen: under twelve seconds.

## 5. Music and sound, concretely

**The cue.** Options, in order of preference:
- A licensed trailer cue from a library you already accept (Artlist, Epidemic, Musicbed): search "dark hybrid strings tanpura", "Indian cinematic tension", 70–80 BPM, 1:30 or longer, with a clear drop and two hits in the last third.
- Pixabay's free cues, which v4 used: "Dark Mystery Trailer (Taking Our Time)" by AlexGrohl is still the right shape. It needs an Indian layer on top: a tanpura drone and one sarangi phrase, both available free on Pixabay and Freesound, mixed under it.
- A commission: a 90-second cue from a composer on Fiverr or SoundBetter costs little and would be ours.

**Diegetic music.** The dhol track for the wedding, cut on its beat (v4's "Midnight Dhol Swagger" from Pixabay, or any sangeet dhol loop).

**Sound layers, from the bottom up:** room tone for each location (bedroom with a fan; the lawn at night with crickets and far traffic; the gate with a generator), object sounds (phone buzz on wood, cloth, glass, a thumb on a screen, a car door), the gunshot and its ring (the game's own, which is good), fire crackle, a riser and a hit from a library, and silence left alone. Mix to −14 LUFS for YouTube, true peak −1.5.

**Voice.** One speaker, recorded close on a phone, then filtered as a WhatsApp voice note (140 Hz to 5 kHz, light compression). Three lines, subtitled in English. If a second voice is wanted for Bhasin, it must be a different real person.

## 6. The one-evening shoot

Everything below is shot in a dark room with one practical lamp and the phone's own light, iPhone on a small tripod, 4K 24 fps, cinematic mode off, exposure locked low. It makes the trailer ours in a way no stock can.

1. A phone face-down on a bedside table, lighting up from beneath. Top-down and three-quarter. Let it buzz on the wood.
2. A hand turning it over; the lock screen with 47 missed calls, in focus, then the hand swiping.
3. A face in the dark lit only by the screen, eyes moving, reading. Two angles. Keep the person anonymous: three-quarter from behind, or eyes only.
4. A thumb scrolling the real game: WhatsApp, the LAPATA poster, a voice note playing. Macro, shallow focus, the room reflected in the glass.
5. The phone on the table beside a laptop with the case file open, hands on the keyboard, the lamp the only light.
6. The phone face-down again, lit from below, one buzz, then dark. The last shot.

Optional, if a second location is possible: a colony gate at night, a parked car with its lights off, a guard's cabin; and a back lawn with fairy lights after everyone has left.

## 7. Stock footage

Pexels, free for commercial use. v4's list is a good base and its IDs are in `raakh-trailer/README.md`: the venue from the air, the dance floor, sparklers, phones up, fireworks, the fog, the roadside, the empty road, the pyre, the hand reaching for a phone, the face lit by a screen, thumbs through chats, 5:00 AM. Add: a colony gate at night, a car waiting with its lights off, a ceiling fan, a bedside table, ash blowing.

Pexels and Pixabay now sit behind a bot check, so the clips have to come through a browser (with your go-ahead for each download) or a free Pexels API key. The old downloads are gone from the machine.

## 8. What stays from v4 and v5

From v4: the card typography and grammar, the warm first act cut on the beat, the gunshot to black, the drop before the title, "Everyone has a version", the end card.
From v5: the compositor as an insert renderer only, the 47-missed-calls cold open as an idea (filmed, not drawn), the "Khana khaya?" tag, the mark-driven build where picture and sound share one timeline.
From neither: synthesised score, synthetic voices, pillarboxed video, forty seconds of reading.

## 9. Order of work

1. Choose the cue. Everything is cut to it.
2. Record the voice lines (three).
3. Shoot the six shots, one evening.
4. Download the stock list.
5. Cut to the music's grid, grade warm then cold, build the sound from the room up.
6. Watch it muted, watch it at five seconds, send it to three people cold.
