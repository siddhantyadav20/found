# What the best trailers do, and what Raakh's should

12 Nov 2026. Research for the next landscape trailer (v5). v4 stays live until v5 is approved.

## 1. The short version

Twelve widely praised trailers were studied, plus interviews with the editors who cut several of them and the data game marketers rely on. Six things recur:

1. **The product's own form is the opening.** The Social Network spent its first minute inside Facebook's interface. Cloverfield was the party video itself. Searching was the screen. Our form is a found iPhone, and v4 never shows it.
2. **One voice or one song carries the whole thing.** Creep, She, Immigrant Song, Smile, Kipling's Boots, Tom Petty, a child's interview in Weapons. Nobody remembers a library cue. v4 runs on a library cue and seventeen cards.
3. **The first five seconds pay off before the slow burn.** YouTube lets people skip at five seconds, Meta counts a view at three, and game buyers scrub the progress bar looking for the real thing. v4's first hard event is at 18 seconds.
4. **Silence does the scaring.** The crickets stop in The Fog, the blackout before the chainsaw, the score dropping out before the title. v4 does this once, well, at the gunshot. It should do it three times.
5. **Tell the premise, withhold the how.** Gone Girl and 12 Years a Slave were cut from the first hour. Andhadhun gave "just enough". The reveal is never touched.
6. **Don't promise a film if the product is a game.** Dead Island's trailer is the most admired game trailer of its decade and the game was panned for not being it. v4 is cinematic drone footage of a wedding; the game is a phone you read at midnight. The gap costs trust at the first tap.

The fix is not a prettier v4. It is a trailer built from the game's own screens, voices and sounds, in the same prestige-thriller grammar v4 already uses.

## 2. The twelve trailers

| # | Trailer | Why it is admired | What we take |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Social Network** (2010, Mark Woollen) | Golden Trailer Best in Show 2011. Opens with about a minute of Facebook photos, status updates and friend confirmations before a frame of the film, under a choral cover of Radiohead's Creep. Woollen: music is "eighty percent of what gives a trailer its pulse". | Open inside the interface. Let a piece of music recontextualise it. |
| 2 | **Gone Girl** (2014) | Richard Butler's off-kilter "She", almost no dialogue, cut from the film's first hour so nothing is spoiled. Ends on Nick's denial to camera, a line that is literally true and double. | Cards in wide-tracked capitals, a warm first act, an ending line that means two things. v4's grammar; keep it. |
| 3 | **The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo** teaser (2011) | Frantic cutting on the beat of Reznor and Karen O's Immigrant Song, text punched in like Gaspar Noé title cards, tagline "The feel-bad movie of Christmas". | 5 | **Cloverfield** teaser (2007) | A home video of a going-away party, interrupted. No title, only a date. Fans went through it frame by frame. | The found-footage form is the trailer. A date as the hook (we have 21 November and seven o'clock). |
| 6 | **Weapons** "Beware" (2025, Mark Woollen & Associates) | Golden Trailer 2026 Best Thriller. | 7 | **Joker** (2019) | Jimmy Durante's "Smile" against the footage, set up by a line of dialogue in the first seconds ("my mother always tells me to smile"). Tonal contrast, not spectacle. | A warm, familiar sound made sinister by what it is laid over. Our dhol track can do this. |
| 8 | **28 Years Later** (2024) | A 1915 recording of Taylor Holmes reading Kipling's Boots, arriving a third of the way in, repeating until it is maddening. Largely dialogue-free. Also read: Adolescence's trailer, which sold the "why" and never mentioned its one-shot gimmick; Bill Neil (Buddha Jones) on YouTube bumpers, silence and rug pulls; the 2003 Texas Chainsaw trailer's off-beat black frames; a Cognitive Science Society study of 21 trailers that found a quarter, half, quarter act structure with shot lengths falling every decade.

## 3. What game marketers add

- **Gameplay in the first seconds.** Derek Lieu's rule for a first trailer. Chris Zukowski's research found people scrub the progress bar for raw gameplay instead of watching from the start. GameDiscoverCo calls the movie-style slow-burn opening the key discovery mistake.
- **"The simpler you make an idea to share, the more it will get shared."** (Lieu.) A friend should be able to repeat the premise in one sentence after one watch. Ours: *a wedding planner's phone came back in the post, and the night a catering boy vanished is on it.*
- **Highs and lows.** "Lows don't look low unless there's a high." (Lieu.) The wedding is the high; keep it, but make it short.
- **Don't obsess over length.** 60 to 90 seconds is where GTA VI, Weapons' second trailer and most Golden Trailer winners sit. v4's 72 seconds is right.
- **Platform mechanics.** YouTube skip at 5 seconds. Meta counts a view at 3 seconds. The most-cited survey has most mobile video watched muted. So: payoff in the first 3 to 5 seconds, burned-in subtitles on every voice, and the trailer must read with the sound off.

## 4. v4 against all that

v4's shape is right and its restraint is right. The problems are in what it is made of.

| v4 as it is | What the research says | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 18 s of stock wedding footage before the first event | Skip at 5 s; scrub for the product | A 4-second cold open of the phone before the wedding |
| No game footage at all (Pexels only) | Social Network, Cloverfield, Searching, Lieu, Dead Island's lesson | At least half the runtime inside the phone's own screens |
| 17 title cards carry the story | Editors call card-and-copy the old template; Weapons used one voice | Cut to 5 cards. Let Sameer, Raju and Bhasin speak |
| Library cue "Dark Mystery Trailer" | Every admired trailer has a signature sound | Build the score from the game: the dhol slowed, the buzz as metronome |
| One silence (the gunshot) | Neil, Woollen: silence before every shock | Three silences: the shot, the deadline, the title |
| No subtitles | Mobile is mostly muted | Burned-in subtitles on every spoken line |
| Phone appears only in the last 3 seconds | The form is the hook | The phone appears at 0:00 and is the frame for everything after the shot |
| The ask is "PLAY FREE IN YOUR BROWSER" | GTA ends on a date, Weapons on a website | End on the URL and the clock: "They want it back by seven." |

Keep from v4: the card typography, the gunshot-to-black, "NO POLICE. NO HOSPITAL. NO BODY.", the drop-out before the title, "Everyone has a version", the buzz in the dark.

## 5. The v5 screenplay (landscape, 75 s)

Built on the quarter, half, quarter structure. Picture is 16:9; inside the phone the game's real 9:16 screens sit on the desk composition the game already renders on desktop (phone centred, case file to the right), so every frame is something the player will actually see.

| Time | Picture | Sound | Card |
|---|---|---|---|

| 4 to 18 | The wedding, but only through phones: the game's Instagram reel UI, the haldi, the drone shot, Kunal's "bhai ye bhi daal dena film mein 🔥🔥", a dozen hands holding phones up. | 18.1 | Hard cut to black | The game's own gunshot. Ears ring. The reel keeps playing, tinny, then stops. | none |
| 19 to 22 | Black | Silence | ONE BOY DIDN'T GO HOME. |
| 22 to 45 | Inside the phone, each shot a punch-in on one thing: the LAPATA poster, "Forwarded many times". "Aap Sameer bhaiya ho na?" Sethi Caterers: "Dilip ka hisaab Bhasin sir ne kar diya". Banyan Security: "Bhasin Sehgal turned on disappearing messages". Locked chats (2). A voice note's waveform moving. The case file on the right filling a blank: "Dilip is a ___, last seen ___". | The score begins from the dhol slowed to a heartbeat; the buzz keeps time. Sameer's voice note to M under it, whole, subtitled. | NO POLICE. / NO HOSPITAL. / NO BODY. (one each, on the booms) |
| 45 to 60 | The clock. Battery 9%, 7%, 5% (the game's true-level battery). An incoming call: Gate 2 · Ram Kishan ji. Cuts accelerate on the buzz. | none |
| 60 to 63 | Black | Everything drops out. One line, quiet: "…is he alive?" | none |
| 63 to 67 | Ash | The hit | RAAKH · CHAPTER ONE |

Twist safety holds: the phone's owner is named only as the game names him in Episode 1; "Sameer bhaiya" and "in your hands" are both literally true; no kada, no reveal screens, no Episode 3 chat.

Shorter cuts from the same edit: a 30-second teaser (0 to 4, 18 to 22, 45 to 52, 60 to 75) for Instagram and the site header, and a 15-second ad (cold open, the poster, the deadline, the title).

## 6. The sound decision

This is the one thing that cannot be assembled from the repo and should be decided first.

- **Option A, built from the game (recommended).** The dhol loop the game already owns, slowed and pitched down until it is a heartbeat; the phone buzz as the metronome, the way A Serious Man's trailer used a head hitting a chalkboard; the gunshot, fire and buzz from `public/sfx`. Nothing to license, and it sounds like nothing else.
- **Option B, a recontextualised song.** A sangeet or lullaby standard slowed down, the Joker and Social Network move. Needs a licence or a public-domain recording and a singer; the most expensive and the most memorable.
- **Option C, a library trailer cue.** What v4 has. Cheapest, and the reason v4 sounds like every other trailer.

## 7. Tests before anything ships

1. **Mute test.** Watch with the sound off. Can a stranger say what it is and what the hook is?
2. **Five-second test.** Stop it at 0:05. Would you keep watching? Stop it at 0:03 as an Instagram feed item.
3. **Scrub test.** Jump to the middle. Is the product on screen?
4. **One-sentence test.** After one watch, a friend repeats the premise in a sentence. If they say "a wedding in Delhi" rather than "a phone", the edit has failed.
5. **Three friends, by phone.** The same protocol as the WhatsApp playtest: send it cold, ask one question, no leading.

## 8. Formats

v4 is already 16:9 (a 2.39:1 picture letterboxed in a 1920×1080 frame). The brief for v5 keeps that, as "landscape". The follow-up cut should be vertical 9:16 for Reels and Status; since every in-game screen is natively 9:16, that cut is mostly a reframe, not a rebuild. Confirm which was meant by "16:9 after that".
